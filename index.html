About Me

I am a researcher working at the intersection of clinical prediction, artificial intelligence, and health data science.

I am interested in the methodological questions that arise when statistical and machine learning models move from development into real clinical settings. My interests include prediction under uncertainty, Bayesian and probabilistic modelling, validation and generalisability, and the evaluation of AI and prediction models across populations and settings.

I am particularly interested in understanding not only whether a model performs, but when its predictions can be trusted, how uncertainty should be represented, why performance changes across settings, and how heterogeneous sources of biomedical information can be combined without hiding what remains unknown.

My background spans AI, medical imaging, signal processing, embedded systems, clinical research, and the development of real-world health data systems. I see these not as separate research identities, but as different ways of approaching problems in measurement, prediction, inference, and decision making.

Research Interests

Clinical Prediction & AI Methodology

Methods for developing, evaluating, and interpreting prediction models and AI systems in healthcare, with particular interest in the assumptions that determine whether apparently strong model performance translates beyond development data.

Uncertainty & Probabilistic Modelling

Quantifying uncertainty in predictions and reasoning under incomplete information. I am interested in Bayesian approaches, probabilistic inference, model uncertainty, and how uncertainty can be communicated meaningfully in clinical prediction.

Validation & Generalisability

Understanding why models perform differently across populations, institutions, and clinical settings, including external validation, calibration, transportability, dataset shift, subgroup performance, and methodological sources of bias.

Multimodal & Longitudinal Modelling

Methods for combining heterogeneous and imperfect information, including clinical measurements, imaging, physiological signals, biomarkers, environmental exposures, and longitudinal observations.

From Methods to Systems

My research interests are shaped partly by having worked on both sides of the modelling pipeline.

Alongside methodological and applied research, I have designed and deployed clinical data streams, research infrastructure, monitoring platforms, and workflow systems in hospital and public-health environments. Building systems that have to operate with real data, real users, and imperfect information has strongly influenced the questions I ask about prediction models and AI.

I am therefore interested in the full path from data and measurement, through modelling and uncertainty, to validation and implementation.

Current Directions

Some questions I am currently interested in include:

  • How should uncertainty around an individual prediction be quantified and communicated?
  • Why do prediction models deteriorate when transported to new populations or settings?
  • How can Bayesian reasoning help when multiple sources of clinical evidence are incomplete, noisy, or conflicting?
  • How should clinical AI systems be evaluated beyond discrimination metrics alone?
  • What can differences between model development and external validation tell us about generalisability?
  • How can heterogeneous longitudinal and multimodal health data be combined without creating unjustified certainty?

These interests are evolving, and this site is intended to document that evolution rather than present them as a fixed research programme.
